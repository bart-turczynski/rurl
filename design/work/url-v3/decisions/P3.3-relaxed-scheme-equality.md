# P3.3 — relaxed scheme equality

## Decision (PROPOSED)

> This record is the agent's **proposed** disposition, offered for the owner to
> ratify, reject, or adjust. Nothing here is binding until the owner merges it
> (P0.1 §4).

### 1. Truth-table row 6's relaxed cells become `equal` (RURL-ixlultql)

Row 6 of the scheme/port truth table
(`contracts/key-join-contracts.md:110`, `HTTP :80` vs `HTTPS :443`) currently
reads `distinct` in **both** relaxed columns. Those two cells are
**unsatisfiable**, not merely awkward. `:66` makes key equality an equivalence
relation, and three other rows of the same table force the pair equal under
transitive closure:

| row | line | pair | `http_https` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `:105` | `HTTP absent` ~ `HTTP :80` | equal |
| 2 | `:106` | `HTTPS absent` ~ `HTTPS :443` | equal |
| 7 | `:111` | `HTTP absent` ~ `HTTPS absent` | equal |

So `HTTP:80` ~ `HTTP absent` ~ `HTTPS absent` ~ `HTTPS:443`. Row 6 denies the
composition of three equalities it does not itself deny.

**Ruled:** row 6's `http_https` and `http_https_missing` cells become `equal`.
The alternative — denying one of rows 1, 2 or 7 — would delete the mode's
purpose, since row 7 *is* the collapse and rows 1-2 are the port rule the
contract states one paragraph earlier.

This is the amendment that made `http_https` implementable. It is recorded here
and **not** applied to the contract text: see §"Affected claims and artifacts".

### 2. `unmatched` is dropped from the required-condition list (RURL-kcgsuzll)

`unmatched` is **not** a condition the join family must raise, and **no**
`unmatched` axis is added. Nothing in the code changes: the six joins already
express unmatched-ness structurally, as `NA` on the non-primary side, which is
what a join *is*. A condition or an axis would re-report the result's own shape
as a diagnostic, and a caller who wants the count already has it
(`is.na(...)` over the key column, or `relationship`).

### 3. `keyability` stays a documented attribute — no ninth export

The typed keyability reason remains reachable as `attr(k, "keyability")` on the
key object, documented, with no accessor of its own. v3's public key/join surface
stays at **eight** names. A ninth export would have to be added to VD-001's
probe list and verified in the same change as the other eight (P0.5 failure
condition 3), buying a wrapper around one `attr()` call.

### 4. `scheme_equality = "http_https"` ships; `http_https_missing` stays refused

With row 6 amended, `http_https` is implementable and **is implemented** in this
slice (`R/url-key.R`, unexported, as the rest of the key engine is).
`http_https_missing` continues to error — but the citation **moves**, because the
reason changed. It is no longer RURL-ixlultql's transitivity clash, which §1
settles for both modes. It is now rows 8 and 11 (RURL-ixxvjjwj), per §"Two cells
this record does not settle".

## Rationale

The collapse is applied to the framed **scheme field**, after the port rule, as a
pure function of that field. Two consequences follow that are worth stating
because they are what makes the mode safe:

**Transitivity is structural, not argued.** Key equality under the collapse is
equality of a projection of the tuple, so it is an equivalence relation *by
construction*, whatever any individual table row says. A future row that
contradicts the closure will therefore be a defect in the row, exactly as row 6
was — the implementation cannot be made non-transitive by a table edit.

**Normalize-before-collapse is load-bearing, and the contract already said so.**
The truth table's own preamble (`:99`) reads: "Default-port normalization occurs
**before** optional scheme collapsing and uses each row's own explicit
recognized scheme." Both orderings fail loudly if swapped, which is why the
sequencing is asserted in tests rather than left to the comment:

- Collapse first, then normalize ports: the port rule matches on
  `scheme == "http"` / `"https"`, so `https://h.com:80/` would see the collapsed
  token, match the `http`+`80` branch, and **lose its `:80`** — breaking row 4
  (`:108`, declared `distinct` in every column).
- Feed the collapsed token to the port rule: neither branch matches, so rows 1-2
  stop normalizing at all.

The token is `http/https`. `/` is outside RFC 3986's `scheme` production
(`[A-Za-z][A-Za-z0-9+.-]*`), so no input can present a real scheme that forges
membership in the collapsed class. A token like `http+https` **would** be
forgeable, since `+` is in the production. This is belt-and-braces — the framed
tuple already leads with `scheme_equality`, so keys minted under different modes
cannot collide — but the token costs nothing to make unspellable.

The relaxed mode widens **no** other scheme family (`:81`, "every other valid
scheme remains exact"). The `ws`/`wss` pair is the one most at risk from a
sloppy collapse, since it shares http/https's default ports exactly; it is
asserted distinct under the relaxed mode in both directions.

## Two cells this record does not settle (RURL-ixxvjjwj)

**Row 8** (`:112`, `missing scheme/no port` vs `HTTP/no port`, declared `equal`
under `http_https_missing`) is not reachable by collapsing scheme + presence.
Measured: `h.com/` and `http://h.com/` also differ on
`authority_delimiter_present` (`F` vs `T`), which P1.2 D-A frames as independent
identity in a SETTLED contract. The declared `equal` needs a **third** collapse
reaching into that contract. If the owner does not want that reach, the honest
alternative is amending the cell to `distinct` — which arguably empties the mode,
since collapsing missing-vs-explicit scheme *is* its entire purpose.

**Row 11** (`:115`, `scheme-relative/no port` vs `missing host-shaped/no port`,
`distinct` in every column) is **posture-dependent**, and the earlier reading
that called it flatly "unevaluable" was too strong. Measured at this baseline:

| standard | `//h.com/` keyable? | row 11 holds by |
|---|---|---|
| `whatwg` | no (`invalid-parse`) | the never-match rule, not key inequality |
| `rfc3986` | **yes** | key inequality on `scheme_presence` (`scheme-relative` vs `inferred`) |

So under `rfc3986` the cell **is** evaluable and does hold. It should not,
however, be re-dispositioned as a clean two-posture verification obligation yet,
because under `rfc3986` **`//p` and `///p` mint byte-identical keys** — filed as
**RURL-kmkyicpt**, and unaffected by the collapse.

> **Correction, stated rather than repaired.** An earlier revision of this
> section called that branch *defective* and attributed it to the key's
> `scheme_policy = "infer"` posture "stripping every leading slash". Both claims
> were wrong and are withdrawn. Nothing strips slashes: `//p` and `///p` are
> prepended with `http:` (`R/parse-phases.R:964-968`) and then converge inside
> `.parse_web_url_one()`, which consumes a run of 1..3 slashes and lets the next
> segment become the authority — libcurl's behavior, reproduced deliberately and
> documented at `R/parse-web.R:70-73`. Under the `rfc3986` selector that
> promotion is **deliberate, tested and sourced**: see the test named "rfc3986
> empty authority does not duplicate host into path"
> (`tests/testthat/test-url-standard-authority.R:72-91`) and the rationale
> carried in `external-url-vectors.csv` ("Section VI.B … rurl profiles now both
> land on evil.com (the fetched/dangerous host)"). Under that posture the shared
> key is *correct*: either input makes a client fetch `http://p/`, and WHATWG
> agrees. Only RFC 3986's grammar separates them, and there the split is unique,
> since `/` is absent from `reg-name`.

`RURL-kmkyicpt` is therefore **a decision, not a bug**: should the `rfc3986`
profile keep the fetched-host slash deviation, or take the grammar? It is routed
to the owner rather than assumed, because it is a *sourced* deviation rather than
incidental legacy, and flipping it changes public parse output under a selector.
Row 11's disposition waits on that answer.

The part that is a defect under **either** answer is split out as
**RURL-xfbzkico**: under `rfc3986`, `http:/evil.com` and `http:////evil.com` are
*rejected*, though the repository's own ABNF oracle
(`tests/testthat/helper-rfc3986-abnf.R`) accepts both and the `whatwg` profile
parses both. A rejection is wrong whichever way the split question resolves. The
cause is the same hard-coded 1..3 bound (`R/parse-web.R:933-942`), applied
regardless of `url_standard` while the neighbouring clauses of that model
(`pqf_bytes`, `host_pct`, `host_charset`, `host_ipv4`) are all already
standard-conditional policies.

With row 6's two amended cells, **6 of the truth table's 42 mode cells** (14 data
rows × 3 mode columns) now carry a disposition the contract text does not yet
reflect: row 6 × 2 relaxed modes, row 8 × `http_https_missing`, row 11 × all
three modes. Those 6 cells span **3** of the 14 rows. Both units are stated
deliberately, because they are easy to conflate: VD-001 counts each table **row**
once, so the outstanding gap there is 3 rows, not 6 cells.

## Consequences

`http_https` is real but unreachable by callers until the export slice lands,
since `url_key_policy()` is not exported yet. That is intended — the mode is
verified through the engine now so that the export slice does not have to
verify both the surface and a new comparison mode at once.

`RURL-ixlultql` and `RURL-kcgsuzll` are dischargeable on merge of this record.
`RURL-ixxvjjwj` is **not**, and now depends on `RURL-kmkyicpt`.
