# P0.8 — Standard/scheme claim ownership

## Decision (PROPOSED)

> This record is the agent's **proposed** disposition, offered for the owner to
> ratify, reject, or adjust. Nothing here is binding until the owner merges it
> (P0.1 §4). The owner has **not** previously spoken on this question. The
> measurements in §2, §3 and §4 are facts; §5 is a proposal.

## 1. The question as asked, and the inference that does not follow

`RURL-lkyverse` asks:

> Add a scheme/profile verification slice, distribute these sections across the
> existing slices, or defer the surface out of G4 scope with recorded impact.

Its stated premise is:

> The eight §7 G4 criterion-3 property families do not include a
> scheme/profile/axis family, so no accepted verification slice owns them.

The first half is **true**: the eight families are `full-string`, `state`,
`vector`, `mutation`, `join`, `cache`, `determinism`, `migration`
(`registers/verification-deferrals.md:59`), and none of them is a scheme,
profile, or axis family.

The second half does **not follow**, and the traceability map refutes it in its
own `## Section ownership` table — using the very decision records SS projects:

- **`HA s2` — "Scheme-forced host posture rows (reference G3.5 / P4.1)" →
  `host-slice`** (`traceability-map.md:185`). The section's own prose says "the
  posture itself is P4.1's" (`host-annotation-contracts.md:101-104`). P4.1 is
  SS's primary source: **31 of SS's 50 rows** cite `P4.1@b017e87`.
- **`KJ s3` — "Scheme and port truth table" → `join-slice`**
  (`traceability-map.md:196`).

So scheme-sourced, P4.1-sourced claims are **already owned** by two different
existing families. The map states the governing principle explicitly at
`:260-263`: *"Cleaning claims follow the cleaning family across contract
boundaries."* Assignment is by **property**, not by contract identity. A claim
about a scheme's host posture is a host claim; a claim about a scheme's default
port is a join claim. Neither needs a scheme family in order to be owned.

A second, independent refutation: **SS claims are already being amended by
sibling records with no slice in existence.** `P1.3` (PROPOSED) amends
`standard-scheme-matrices.md:131` — the `TR-SS-s4-authority-emission-binds-p1-2`
claim — directly (`P1.3:205`). Ownership of a claim's *semantics* and ownership
of its *evidence* are different axes, and the absence of a scheme family has
never blocked the first.

## 4. The blocker is the map's **granularity**, not its family list

Ownership in the map is assigned **per section**. Three SS sections cannot be
expressed at that granularity, and this — not the missing scheme family — is
what actually stalls the assignment.

**`SS s5` (9 claims) spans four families in one table.** Its columns are
`family | special-ness (whatwg) | default port | host / PSL eligibility |
semantic-transform eligibility` (`standard-scheme-matrices.md:135`). By the
map's own precedents those belong to four different owners: default port →
`join-slice` (KJ s3), host/PSL eligibility → `host-slice` (HA s2, HA s4),
semantic-transform eligibility → `mutation-slice` (CM s2), special-ness → the
route property of §5 D-D below. Any single `owning_slice` value for s5 is
wrong for at least six of its nine claims.

**A boundary row does not migrate — verified, because the tempting reading is
the opposite one.** `SS s6` contains `credential output policy (boundary)`,
status `SETTLED (boundary)`, whose text says "owned by **P2.2**,
cross-referenced not redefined" (`:153`). It is tempting to route it to
`full-string-slice`, which owns the OUT contract. The map's precedent says
otherwise, and distinguishes two cases:

- A boundary **row inside** a section follows its section's family:
  `output-contracts.md:94` is a `SETTLED (boundary)` row reading "**owned by
  G3.K**, referenced", it sits inside `## Five output surfaces` (lines 82-95 =
  `OUT s1`), and `OUT s1 → full-string-slice` — *not* `join-slice`.
- A boundary **section** goes to the referenced family: `OUT s8`
  ("Comparison key (surface e) — reference to G3.K") `→ join-slice`.

So SS s6's boundary row stays with SS s6. Recorded because a future slice author
following the intuitive rule would silently move a claim to the wrong owner, and
the population rule counts that row as a claim either way (its status cell
begins with `SETTLED`).

## 5. The decision

**D-A. No scheme/profile verification slice is created.** Not because a ninth
family is barred — the map's bar at `:847-850` is on inventing a ninth *family*,
and `release-slice`/`host-slice` show non-family registry entries are
admissible (the P0.7 §1(b) correction) — but because measurement shows the
premise for one is false: scheme claims already distribute by property across
`host-slice` and `join-slice` today (§1).

**D-B. The population is dispositioned as assignment, not deferral.** All 50
surfaces ship (§2). No `VD-nnn` row is minted for any SS claim, and the issue's
third branch (defer out of G4 scope with recorded impact) is declined as
inapplicable rather than rejected on merit.

**D-C. The four `OPEN` cells need nothing from this record.** Their
destinations are contract-named and the G3.X criterion-(v) census already
ratifies them as owned (§2).

**D-D. Ownership for `SS s5` — and for any section whose columns cross families
— becomes claim-granular.** The map already mints stable per-claim ids
(`TR-<contract>-s<n>-<slug>`, `:119-124`) and the generated claim index already
carries a per-claim `owning_slice` column, so the *key* exists. But that column
is **derived, not assignable**: the index is a generated block the gate
"compares byte-for-byte against a fresh derivation from the contracts"
(`:267-268`), and its `owning_slice` value is a projection of the section-level
`## Section ownership` row. Claim-granular ownership therefore requires the
generator to read a **claim-level override** alongside the section table — a
change to `traceability-gate.R`'s derivation, not only a map edit. That cost is
stated rather than assumed: an earlier draft of this record asserted it "costs
no new machinery", which the index header falsifies. The override table is the
substantive change the ticket's question actually requires, and it is small —
one row per claim that dissents from its section — but it is a tool change and
must be built with the gate proved red first.

**D-E. The genuine residue is `s1`, `s2`, `s3`, and the two `SETTLED` input-axis
rows of `s8` — and its property is *admission*, not *scheme*.** These sections
(axis lattice and profile expansion, precedence, the admission gate, scheme-less
and scheme-relative input) do not describe a property of a parsed URL. They
describe **which parse runs at all**. That is a precondition of every one of the
eight families rather than a property of any one of them, which is exactly why
no family names it — and exactly why minting a slice for it would repeat the
`vector-slice` finding the map already reports (`:866-874`): a named category
that certifies nothing, because the claims are discharged inside every other
family's fixtures rather than by a slice of their own. Whether that residue is
verified by an admission *fixture obligation* laid on the other slices, or by a
registry entry of its own, is the owner call this ticket should have asked.
Per-claim tallies for the assigned sections are the assigning edit's job, not
this record's — the P0.7 precedent on counting coverage only where it is
measured.

## 7. Non-scope

- **No contract cell is touched.** Every SS cell keeps its status and its
  `owner_decision_ref`. This record decides ownership of *evidence*, never
  semantics, and settles none of SCHEME-O1..O4.
- **No map row is edited by this record.** `UNASSIGNED` is, in the map's own
  words, "a factual statement that no registered slice's family covers a
  section" and explicitly not a deferral (`:844-846`). Flipping SS rows while
  this record is PROPOSED would make the map assert an ownership the owner has
  not ratified — the P1.3, P2.6 and P0.7 precedent. The map edits, the census
  regeneration, and D-D's granularity change ride the seal PR.
- **No `VD-nnn` row is registered** (D-B).
- **No gate or tool is modified *by this record*.** D-D does, however, *imply* a
  `traceability-gate.R` change: the claim index's `owning_slice` column is
  generated from the section table, so a claim-level override is a derivation
  change (D-D). This record neither writes it nor pre-approves its shape;
  whoever builds it must make the generator go red on a dissenting claim before
  trusting its green.
- **`RURL-ogktvhgp` is not pre-empted.** §3 measures that the gates'
  disagreement does not affect this population; it does not resolve it.
- **No implementation is scheduled.** Whether SCHEME-O1's precedence foot-gun or
  SCHEME-O4's resolution layer should be *built* is product authority
  (P2/P4), untouched here.
- **G4's exit is not moved.** P0.5 governs that; this record only makes 50 of
  its claims legible.

## Open question this record does **not** answer

D-E names the residue's property (admission — "which parse runs at all") and
argues a slice for it would certify nothing, but it does **not** decide how that
residue is verified. Two shapes are available and the choice is the owner's:
lay an **admission-fixture obligation** on each of the eight families, so every
slice must exercise its property across the admission matrix; or add a registry
entry that owns the matrix once and is cited by the others. The first keeps the
family list fixed and risks eight partial copies of one matrix; the second is a
non-family registry entry on the `host-slice` precedent and risks the
`vector-slice` nullity. This is the original question sharpened: **not** "does
G4 need a scheme slice", but "is admission a property to be verified once or a
precondition to be exercised eight times".
