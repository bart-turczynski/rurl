# P2.6 — The VAL-O1 recoveries are standard-selected parse behavior

## Decision (PROPOSED)

> This record is the agent's **proposed** disposition, offered for the owner to
> ratify, reject, or adjust. Nothing here is binding until the owner merges it
> (P0.1 §4).

## 1. What P2.1 Q5 deferred

P2.1 categorized the fixer steps, scheme inference, and the repeated-`@`
recovery, then stopped:

> **Q5 — full recovery-scope categorization — DEFERRED.** Beyond repeated-`@`,
> the per-row `strict`-excluded vs parser-behavior assignment for the other
> ledger recoveries is **deferred to the RCON-04 intervention-ledger work at
> G3**.

`contracts/validation-intervention-contract.md` projects that deferral as the
`ledger completeness` row of the ordered-intervention-ledger table and as open
cell **VAL-O1**, enumerating four recoveries from frozen evidence S2 (`:75`,
`:246`): WHATWG IPv4 rewriting, the host-charset shim (ADR 0009), curl PQF
sanitization, and dependency shims.

The question was **unanswerable when it was asked**, and not for want of
deliberation. All four were compensations that ran *outside* an engine the
package did not own. A compensation that rewrites the input string in front of
a parser genuinely sits on the repair axis — it is a byte-changing pre-parse
pass, which is the definition of pipeline stage 1. Asking "is this
`strict`-excluded or is it parser behavior?" of code that runs before the parser
has no principled answer, because the thing it is compensating for is invisible
from where it stands.

## 2. What changed: the compensation layer no longer exists

`RURL-ezhzpkhg` (five deletions) and `RURL-robgajml` (the in-tree parser) moved
every one of the four out of the pre-parse layer. Measured at `5ec1edd`:

| VAL-O1 recovery (S2 era) | where it is now | seam |
|---|---|---|
| WHATWG IPv4 rewriting | parser dial `host_ipv4` | `.web_host_ipv4_policy()` (`R/parse-web.R:392`) |
| host-charset shim (ADR 0009) | parser dial `host_charset` | `.web_host_charset_policy()` (`R/parse-web.R:362`) |
| curl PQF sanitization | parser dial `pqf_bytes` | `.web_pqf_policy()` (`R/parse-web.R:381`) |
| dependency shims | **extinct** — `curl` is not in `Imports:` (`DESCRIPTION:39-43`) | — |

Each surviving dial is a formal of `.parse_web_url_one()` (`R/parse-web.R:903`)
and each is derived from **`opts$url_standard` and nothing else**
(`R/parse.R:1788-1812`). ADR 0013 (superseding ADR 0009) records the same
conclusion for the host-charset half: acceptance belongs to the parser, on its
own dial.

## 3. Why the question is now answerable

Because the axis is visible. The four are selected by **`url_standard`** — the
standard-selection axis — and P2.1 §1's postures are the **repair** axis. Two
orthogonal axes, and the deferred question presupposed they were one.

That is why `strict` never excluded them: `strict` is defined by P2.1 §1 as "no
repair, no recovery; raw bytes flow through standards-required preprocessing
into a strict parse". A dial that decides what the *selected standard's parse*
accepts is inside "a strict parse", not upstream of it. Nothing about it is
posture-scoped, and the load-bearing invariant — "under `strict`, pipeline
stages 1 and 4 are a tested no-op" — is untouched, because these are stage 5.

## 4. The decision

**D-A. The four VAL-O1 recoveries are standard-selected parse behavior,
governed by `url_standard`, not by the repair posture.** They execute in
pipeline **stage 5** (strict parse + component extraction), whose RCON-04
category is already `n/a (parse)`. No posture enables or disables any of them.

**D-B. None of them is `strict`-excluded.** All are reachable under every
posture, including `strict`, for exactly the standard the caller selected. A
posture never overrides a standard's own grammar; where a caller wants the
narrower grammar they select the narrower standard, which is a different
argument.

**D-C. None of them produces an intervention-ledger row, because none of them
is an intervention.** The RCON-04 ledger records what a *posture* did to the
input on its way to the parse — stages 1–4. Where a parsed component's spelling
differs from the input's (IPv4 canonicalization, `%xx` uppercasing, high-byte
percent-encoding, WHATWG's C0/SP/DEL escape), that is the selected standard's
own rendering of a parse result, and it is owned by the host and output
contracts (**G3.H**, **P2.2 / G3.7**) — boundaries this contract already
declares — not by the input-side ledger.

**D-D. The `ledger completeness` cell therefore settles as "no rows", not as an
assignment.** The ordered intervention ledger stays at six stages. VAL-O1 closes
by recording that its four entries were re-homed out of the ledger's domain, not
by categorizing four new rows.

**D-E. "Dependency shims" is retired as a category, not answered.** `curl` is
gone; there is no dependency to shim. Should rurl ever again adapt around a
dependency's behavior, that adaptation is a **new** finding requiring its own
categorization — D-A does not pre-authorize one.

## 5. Evidence

Measured at `5ec1edd`, one dial varied at a time against
`.parse_web_url_one()` (all other formals at their defaults), so no result is
confounded by a sibling dial:

| dial | input | value → parsed |
|---|---|---|
| `host_charset` | `http://a!b.com/p` | `narrow` → REJECT · `rfc3986` → `a!b.com` · `whatwg` → `a!b.com` |
| `host_ipv4` | `http://0X7f.1/p` | `narrow` → `0X7f.1` · `whatwg` → `127.0.0.1` |
| `host_ipv4` | `http://127.0.0.1./p` | `narrow` → `127.0.0.1.` · `whatwg` → `127.0.0.1` |
| `host_ipv4` | `http://0x.0x.0/p` | `narrow` → `0x.0x.0` · `whatwg` → `0.0.0.0` |
| `pqf_bytes` | `http://h.com/a b` | `reject` → REJECT · `encode` → path `/a%20b` |
| `pqf_bytes` | `http://h.com/%2f%c3%a9` | `reject` → `/%2F%C3%A9` · `encode` → `/%2F%C3%A9` (**identical**) |

And the policy seams themselves, read at `5ec1edd`:

| `url_standard` | `host_pct` | `host_charset` | `pqf_bytes` | `host_ipv4` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `NULL` (baseline) | `decode` | `narrow` | `reject` | `narrow` |
| `"rfc3986"` | `keep` | `rfc3986` | `reject` | `narrow` |
| `"whatwg"` | `decode` | `whatwg` | `encode` | `whatwg` |

Two of those rows correct the framing this record was proposed under
(`RURL-ztuodtcl`), and both corrections narrow the claim rather than widen it:

1. **`host_charset` and `host_ipv4` are acceptance/recognition dials, not
   rewrite dials.** `host_charset` changes *which hosts parse* and mutates
   nothing. `host_ipv4` changes *which tokens read as an address*; the
   canonicalization that follows is present under **both** its values —
   `0x7f.1` renders as `127.0.0.1` under `narrow` too — so the canonicalization
   is baseline host serialization, and the dial only widens what reaches it.
   Only `pqf_bytes = "encode"` introduces a byte the input did not write, and
   only for C0/SP/DEL: the last table row shows the high-byte encoding and
   `%xx` uppercasing are unconditional parse behavior, identical under both
   values.
2. **The category is stage 5's `n/a (parse)`, not stage 2's
   `standards-required preprocessing`.** Stage 2 is a closed, enumerated list —
   WHATWG control-character stripping, literal-backslash recognition, UTS-46
   separator mapping — run as step 1 of the parse. These four are the parse's
   own grammar and encode sets, one stage later.

## 6. Non-scope

- **No host, IDNA, IP-literal or PSL semantics.** How a recognized IPv4 address
  is spelled back is G3.H's (RCON-08). D-C names the boundary; it does not cross
  it.
- **No output/serialization rule.** P2.2 / G3.7.
- **No public surface.** No new argument, no widened parse frame, no new
  diagnostic token. `host-charset-shimmed` (`R/diagnostics.R:287`) keeps its
  current meaning: it describes the resulting host, and survived the shim it was
  named after (`R/parse.R:1858-1873`).
- **No posture spelling.** VAL-O2 / P2.1 Q4 / RCON-05 stay open, untouched.
- **No behavior change.** This record proposes a categorization of behavior that
  already exists; it asks for no code edit and no test change.
- **P2.1 is not edited.** It is ACCEPTED and hash-pinned
  (`schema/record-schemas.yaml:41`, `append_only_when: ACCEPTED`). This record
  completes its Q5 from outside, exactly as Q5's own destination anticipated.

## 7. One observation this record deliberately does not decide

`last_at_userinfo` — the stage-4 repeated-`@` recovery P2.1 §3 C-03 binds to
`compatibility`/`repair` and **excludes from `strict`** — is today driven by
`.is_whatwg(opts$url_standard)` (`R/parse.R:1783`), i.e. by the standard
selector, because the posture axis is not implemented yet. That is a known gap
between C-03's v3 target and shipped code, not a contradiction of it, and not a
reason to reopen a SETTLED cell. It is recorded here so the gap is written down
where the categorization work will look for it; closing it belongs to whichever
slice first implements the posture argument (VAL-O2 / RCON-05).
