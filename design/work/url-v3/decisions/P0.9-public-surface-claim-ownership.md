# P0.9 — Public-surface claim ownership

## Decision (PROPOSED)

> This record is the agent's **proposed** disposition, offered for the owner to
> ratify, reject, or adjust. Nothing here is binding until the owner merges it
> (P0.1 §4). The owner has **not** previously spoken on this question. The

## 1. The question as asked, and the two facts that moved beneath it

`RURL-sunrlgio` asks the owner to

> Rule whether meta-claims belong in the criterion-1 population at all,

and offers two branches: a `TRANSITIVE` disposition distinct from `UNASSIGNED`
plus "a rule that the referenced owning contract is itself covered"; or a
documented carve-out from the population rule, "which must be narrow, because
the population rule's whole value is that it is mechanical and nothing escapes
by hand."

The framing is sound and the reason for the constraint is right. Two of the
issue's stated facts, however, are no longer true — both because P0.6 landed
between the filing and now.

**The four sections no longer live in one contract.** The issue names them as
sections of `public-surface-closure.md`. P0.6 split artifact 4 into an invariant
half and a roster half, and three of the four moved:
`## Bijection`, `## Exported-function disposition roster` and
`## Public-output-field disposition roster` are now
`public-surface-disposition.md:78`, `:97`, `:133`, while
`## Cross-artifact term / status / state-field agreement` stayed at
`public-surface-closure.md:155`. The population therefore spans **two**
contracts, and — as §3 and §5 show — the split is not cosmetic: the two halves
have different gate status, different instruments, and different right answers.

**No circulating count is correct.** The issue title says 55; its own 2026-07-25
correction comment says 59; summing the generated census rows for the two
contracts gives 65. Derived from the generated claim index at `300370c`:

| section | claims |
|---|---|
| `PS s1` — Cross-artifact term / status / state-field agreement | 8 |
| `PSD s1` — Bijection | 4 |
| `PSD s2` — Exported-function disposition roster | 31 |
| `PSD s3` — Public-output-field disposition roster | 18 |
| **UNASSIGNED total** | **61** |

65 is the sum of both contracts including `PSD s4` (4 claims), which is already
owned by `migration-slice` (`traceability-map.md:217`). 59 is the pre-split
arithmetic with `s2` at 29 exports. 55 is older still. The correct answer is
**61**, and the reason all three drifted is the reason the map generates its own
index: a transcribed count is a stale fact waiting to happen.

**The premise that the sections are meta-claims is true.** All four carry rows
that assert something *about* other artifacts — an inventory (`PSD s1`), two
ownership rosters (`PSD s2/s3`), and eight cross-artifact agreements (`PS s1`) —
rather than a runtime property of a parse. Nothing below disputes it. What §5
disputes is that they are therefore *one* population.

## 2. Measurement: the TRANSITIVE branch fails on its own terms

The branch says a meta-claim is discharged when "the referenced owning contract
is itself covered." Both readings of that were measured against the map's
section-ownership table at `300370c`.

**Strict reading — every contract a row names is itself fully covered.** A
contract is covered when every one of its sections resolves to a slice that is
`SHIPPED` on disk. Exactly one of the ten qualifies: `SC` (all five sections →
`cache-slice`, the only shipped slice that owns any section). So of the 49
roster rows in `PSD s2/s3`, **3 discharge** — `rurl_cache_config`,
`rurl_cache_info`, `rurl_clear_caches` — and 46 do not. Of the 61 claims, 3.
The rule is not wrong; it is inert, and it stays inert until six owed slices
ship.

**Generous reading — resolve each row through its named contract to a slice.**
Mapping each row's `owning contract(s)` cell through the legend
(`public-surface-closure.md:141-153`) to the map's section→slice rows:

| outcome | rows |
|---|---|
| determinate — exactly one slice | 14 |
| ambiguous — 2, 3, or 4 distinct slices | 22 |
| resolves into `UNASSIGNED` | 13 |
| **not resolvable to one owner** | **35 of 49** |

Ambiguity is structural, not incidental: `G3.7` alone spans three slices
(`OUT s6` → `mutation-slice`, `OUT s8` → `join-slice`, the rest →
`full-string-slice`), and `G3.K` spans two (`KJ s7` → `migration-slice`). One
row, the `path` field, resolves to four. This is P0.8 D-D's granularity finding
arriving from the opposite direction: there, one section spanned four families
in its columns; here, one named contract spans four slices in the map.

**And the rule is circular against its own siblings.** 13 rows resolve into
`UNASSIGNED` because the contracts they name — `G3.5` (all nine sections
`UNASSIGNED`, carrier `RURL-lkyverse`) and `G3.6` (nine of ten, carrier
`RURL-jdnlpydz`) — are the other two members of this very finding. A transitive
rule adopted today would report 13 of these claims as discharged-by-a-section-
that-is-itself-undischarged.

**Two rows point outside the map entirely.** `query_param_summary` and
`safe_parse_urls` name `§6 artifact 11`, which is the verification family
itself and carries no rows in the contract population. And one `PS s1` row names
`ADR 0006 (via G3.6)` as its canonical owner — an ADR, not a §6 contract. A
transitive lookup over contracts cannot resolve either.

## 3. Measurement: the carve-out branch costs a G3 reopening for no semantic change

The map already has an exclusion mechanism, `## Excluded sources`
(`traceability-map.md:797`), and it already carries exactly one entry:
`cross-artifact-consistency.md`, excluded because "its rows are re-checkable
assertions **about** the other nine contracts … Its assertions are verified
where they are written." That is the closest available precedent, and it is a
real one. But the mechanism cannot be pointed at either half of this population.

**It is file-granular, and claim-bearing-ness is derived.** `T6`
(`traceability-gate.R:564-584`) computes the zero-claim contracts from the
contract text and then requires that every listed file *actually* carry no
claims (`wrongly <- listed[listed %in% ...unique(claims$contract)]`). A file
cannot be listed while it has a table whose last cell begins with `SETTLED` or
`OPEN`. So excluding `PS s1` means editing `public-surface-closure.md` to stop
carrying a status column in that table.

**That file is a G3 `## Inputs` pin** — hash-recorded in both
`gates/G3-acceptance.md:82` and `gates/G3-acceptance-2.md:87`, and the file's
own header says it "stays a G3 `## Inputs` pin and changes only when the
ownership model changes — which is exactly when G3 SHOULD reopen." A
presentational edit that removes a status column changes no ownership model, so
it would spend a reopening on nothing. The honest refinement: that pin is
**already** red at this baseline (`validate-records.R` reports the drift for
`public-surface-closure.md`, recorded `9b4fdb2d3bc6`, got `9bc15ab8a73a`, from
P0.6's own split), so the cost is not a *new* red — it is that the reopening
rule would fire for a reason it was written to exclude.

**And `PSD` cannot be excluded at all.** It carries 57 claims, four of them
already owned by `migration-slice` (`PSD s4`). File-granular exclusion is not
merely expensive there, it is unavailable.

## 4. Measurement: a roster row is a pointer, and its referent is already in the population

The roster's rows do not state independent propositions; they restate the owning
contract's, plus a forward reference. Concretely, `domain` appears three times
in the population:

| claim | owner | status | text |
|---|---|---|---|
| `TR-CS-s1-domain` (`canonical-state-contract.md:130`) | `state-slice` | SETTLED | "registrable domain in the `host_encoding` spelling … an L3 annotation output, never allowed to alter L1/L2 (P1.1 §2)" |
| `TR-HA-s1-public-host-type-syntactic-form` (`host-annotation-contracts.md:99`) | `host-slice` | SETTLED | the `host_type = "domain"` overload, "de-overloading OPEN — HOST-O2" |
| `TR-PSD-s3-domain` (`public-surface-disposition.md:146`) | `UNASSIGNED` | **OPEN** | "L3 PSL annotation in `host_encoding` spelling (P1.1@a7e0a59 §2); `domain` overload → HOST-O2" |

The third is the conjunction of the first two. Note its status *differs*: it is
`OPEN` because it folds in the `HOST-O2` forwarding, while both referents are
`SETTLED`. A verification slice authored to discharge `TR-PSD-s3-domain` would
have to re-derive evidence that `state-slice` and `host-slice` already owe — the
second-writer drift surface the map's own `## Scope boundaries` says it exists
to prevent ("duplicating evidence citations would create a second writer and a
new drift surface, which is the failure this artifact exists to prevent").

This generalizes: **every** one of the 49 roster rows names at least one contract
whose sections are already in the population, so there is no roster row whose
substance is absent from it.

## 5. Measurement: the map already has a qualified-`UNASSIGNED` subtype — in one spelling only

The issue asks for "a `TRANSITIVE` disposition distinct from `UNASSIGNED`." A
mechanism for a distinct disposition **already exists in the gate**, undocumented
in the map. Two spellings were tested by editing the four ownership rows in a
scratch worktree of `300370c`, regenerating, and running the gate.

| spelling | T0–T7 | by-slice census |
|---|---|---|
| `UNASSIGNED[criterion-1]` | **PASS** (8/8) | complete — `UNASSIGNED` stays 173, column sums to the 483 total; the qualifier is visible per claim in the index |
| `UNASSIGNED (criterion-1 self-verified)` | **PASS** (8/8) | **61 claims vanish** — `UNASSIGNED` drops 173 → 112, column sums to 422 against a `**total**` of 483 |

The cause is a spelling mismatch between two functions. `T2` accepts any owner
with `startsWith(owner, "UNASSIGNED")` (`traceability-gate.R:484`), and so does
`resolve_coverage` (`:338`) — but `generate_census` groups with
`r$owner == o | startsWith(r$owner, paste0(o, "["))` (`:360-366`), which folds
back **only** the bracket form. A parenthesized qualifier is therefore accepted
by the vocabulary rule, resolved to `coverage = UNASSIGNED` in the index, and
then silently dropped from every census group. `T3` cannot catch it: the census
is generated, so the block agrees byte-for-byte with the generator, and both are
wrong together. This is the `RURL-ogktvhgp` failure mode — gates passing while
contradicting each other — reproduced on demand.

**The bracket form is the mechanism, and it costs nothing.** It needs no gate
change, no new vocabulary rule, and no census change. Its honest limitation:
`generate_census` folds it into the parent `UNASSIGNED` group, so the subtype is
recorded per claim in the index but is **not tallied**. Reporting "61 of 173 are
not criterion-3 gaps" needs a generator change — in the same generator P0.8 D-D
already requires (`RURL-sbhpzwzk`). The two siblings converge on one leaf.

## 6. Measurement: the two halves have different instruments, and that is the answer

`validate-records.R` treats the two halves of artifact 4 differently, and the
difference is exactly the line the disposition should follow.

**Section G (`:933-1067`), over the roster, enforces I1–I5 executably on every
run.** It derives the export set from `NAMESPACE` and the field set from
`.spu_result_fields` and checks the roster against them by name in both
directions (I1/I2); it checks the bijection table's components against the
derived surface (I1); it reads the legend out of the invariant half and requires
every row's owning contract to be in it (I2); it requires every `SETTLED` row to
cite a `P<n>.<n>@<sha>`, an ADR, or its discharge (I3); and for every `OPEN` row
it resolves the cited open-cell id **into the contract that row names**, failing
on a dangling citation (I3/I4). It passes today: all 6 failures at this baseline
are `## Inputs`/manifest hash drift, and none is one of these conformance
checks — the one that *names* `public-surface-closure.md` (evidence 12) is a
recorded-vs-computed sha256 mismatch, not an I1–I5 violation.

That instrument discharges `PSD s1/s2/s3` — 53 claims — more directly than a
verification slice would, because it re-derives the population rather than
citing it.

**Section F (`:908-931`), over the invariant, does not read the agreement
table at all.** It checks that no roster table has crept back in, that clauses
I1–I5 are stated, that no `## Bijection` table lives in the pinned file, and
that the legend maps ≥8 leaves to files that exist. It never looks at
`## Cross-artifact term / status / state-field agreement`. `PS s1`'s 8 claims
have **no instrument** — and the section's own text says why: "full
cross-artifact enforcement is the G3.X capstone; this section is the
public-surface slice of it."

So the exclusion precedent's reason — "verified where they are written" — is
**true of the capstone and false of `PS s1`**. The capstone's five criterion
rows are checked (`validate-records.R:1069-1078`, every verdict must be `PASS`);
`PS s1`'s eight are not. Sweeping the 8 in with the 53 would file a real gap as
self-verified.

## 7. The decision

**D-A — The population is 61 claims across two contracts, derived.** `PS s1`
(8) + `PSD s1` (4) + `PSD s2` (31) + `PSD s3` (18). The issue's 55, its
correction's 59, and the two-contract census sum of 65 are each wrong; 65
includes `PSD s4`, already owned by `migration-slice`. The map's
`## Open cells` 1 carries 59 for this carrier and is corrected at the seal.

**D-B — Reject the `TRANSITIVE` branch as specified.** Not on principle but on
measurement (§2): the strict reading discharges 3 of 49 roster rows and stays
inert until six owed slices ship; the generous reading leaves 35 of 49 without a
single owner; 13 resolve into the two sibling populations, making the rule
circular; and three rows name owners the map does not carry at all (§6 artifact
11 ×2, ADR 0006 ×1). A rule whose answer for 71% of its subject is "ambiguous"
is not a disposition.

**D-C — Reject the carve-out branch as specified.** `T6`'s exclusion is
file-granular and derives claim-bearing-ness from the contract text, so
excluding `PS s1` requires a presentational edit to a file hash-pinned in both
G3 acceptance records — spending a reopening on a change to no ownership model —
and `PSD` cannot be excluded at all while it carries 57 claims and an assigned
`s4` (§3). The issue's instinct that a carve-out "must be narrow" is right; the
narrowest available carve-out is still a file.

**D-D — `PSD s1/s2/s3` (53 claims) are criterion 1's own extension, and their
instrument already exists and runs.** They take
`owning_slice = UNASSIGNED[criterion-1]` with carrier `RURL-sunrlgio`, meaning:
*no criterion-3 property family covers this section because the section is not a
criterion-3 claim — it is the extension of criterion 1 itself, and
`validate-records.R` §G enforces it on every run.* This is measured to require
no gate change (§5). It is not a deferral, mints no `VD-nnn` row, and asserts no
coverage: the claims stay in the population and stay outside the eight families,
which is the truth about them.

**D-E — `PS s1` (8 claims) is a different case and must not be swept in.** It
takes `owning_slice = UNASSIGNED[capstone]` with carrier `RURL-jzgelfto`, the
leaf that extends the capstone's own validator over its eight agreement rows
(§6).
Its property is cross-artifact agreement, its owner is G3.X, and it has no
instrument today. Calling it self-verified would be false; calling it a
criterion-3 gap would send it to a family that does not cover it.

**D-F — The parenthesized spelling is a gate defect, independent of this
disposition.** `UNASSIGNED (…)` passes all eight rules while removing its claims
from every census group (173 → 112, column sum 422 against a total of 483). It
should be closed by making the two functions agree — either `T2` rejecting any
qualifier that `generate_census` cannot group, or the grouper accepting the same
prefix set `T2` does. Filed as `RURL-fymdhizq`; D-D and D-E use the bracket form
precisely because it is the spelling that is measured to work.

## 9. Non-scope

- **No contract semantic is decided.** Every roster row keeps the owning
  contract and SETTLED/OPEN disposition P0.6 gave it. No `OPEN` cell is settled
  and no `PSC-O` group is regrouped.
- **No map row, register row, contract cell, or gate line is edited by this
  record.** P1.3, P2.6, P0.7 and P0.8 all follow the same posture; the edits ride
  the seal PR. In particular the four `owning_slice` values D-D and D-E propose
  are *proposed*, not applied — the gate stays exactly as green as it was.
- **No new property family.** D-D and D-E both keep the eight fixed; neither
  claims a ninth, and `UNASSIGNED[...]` is explicitly not a slice name.
- **No deferral.** D-D and D-E mint no `VD-nnn` row. P0.7's shipped-ness axis
  does not apply here for a different reason than it failed for `SS`: the
  surfaces these claims describe are the *entire* shipped public API, so
  unshippedness — the only thing that justifies a deferral
  (`verification-deferrals.md:44-47`) — is unavailable by construction.
- **The gate defect is named, not fixed.** D-F records the spelling mismatch and
  its measurement; the repair is a separate leaf, because a PROPOSED record that
  also patched the gate would be its own evidence.
- **`RURL-sbhpzwzk` is not absorbed.** That leaf teaches the claim-index
  generator a claim-level assignment override (P0.8 D-D). The tally D-D's
  limitation asks for is a census-grouping change in the same file. Related, not
  the same change.

## Open question this record does **not** answer

`UNASSIGNED[criterion-1]` records that criterion 1's instrument covers these
claims — but the claims are rows *of the roster criterion 1 quantifies over*, so
criterion 1 is in some measure measuring itself. §4 shows the self-reference is
benign in substance (each row's referent is independently owned elsewhere in the
population), and §3 shows the alternative costs a semantically empty G3
reopening. What it does not settle is whether the owner would rather pay that
cost once, at the seal, and have the population contain only claims that
something outside itself verifies. That is a question about what criterion 1 is
*for*, and only the owner can answer it.

Second, smaller: D-E routes `PS s1` to `RURL-jzgelfto`, which extends the
capstone's validator while the rows stay where they are. If the owner
prefers, `PS s1`'s eight rows could instead move into
`cross-artifact-consistency.md` — where the instrument already lives — which
would make them capstone rows rather than public-surface rows and let the
existing exclusion cover them. That is a contract edit to two G3-pinned files,
so it is a bigger change than it looks, and it is not proposed here.
