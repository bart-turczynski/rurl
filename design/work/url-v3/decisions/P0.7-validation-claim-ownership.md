## Decision (PROPOSED)

> This record is the agent's **proposed** disposition, offered for the owner to
> ratify, reject, or adjust. Nothing here is binding until the owner merges it
> (P0.1 §4). Unlike P0.5 — which made durable a decision the owner had already

## 1. The question as asked, and why its binary is incomplete

`RURL-jdnlpydz` asks:

> Does the state slice widen to cover the verdict/repair surface, or does G4
> gain a validation slice?

Both branches assume the same thing: that the 62 claims form one block needing
one owner, and that the only open variable is *which* slice's name goes in the
`owning_slice` column. Three measured facts contradict that assumption.

**(a) The 62 are not one block.** They split cleanly by whether the surface
they describe exists in the package — 37 shipped, 25 not (§2). Under P0.5 those
two halves are not the same kind of question. A cell whose surface is shipped
needs an owning slice and evidence. A cell whose surface is *unbuilt* is
explicitly **not** a slice-assignment problem: P0.5 fixes its only admissible
dispositions as "defer with a named carrier" or "block G4", and routes it to
`registers/verification-deferrals.md`, not to the ownership table. Assigning an
unbuilt cell to a slice would create precisely the false coverage claim P0.5
§"Deferral is not satisfaction" forbids.

**(b) The premise that no property family covers repair is false as stated.**
The issue says none of the eight §7 families "covers repair/intervention or
resolution". But `traceability-map.md:181` already assigns **CM s6, "Cleaning
repair-provenance rows (S4 F3)", to `mutation-slice`** — repair provenance,
owned by a family, today. The mutation family covers repair provenance on the
cleaning side. What is absent is not a family that can host repair claims; it
is a *shipped repair surface* for the input side to make claims about.

**(c) A "validation slice" would not be a ninth family, and is not blocked.**
`traceability-map.md:847-850` says the eight families are "Fixed by §7 G4
criterion 3 … not license to invent a ninth family", which reads as a bar on
the issue's second branch. It is not. The slice registry already contains
**two** entries that are not property families — `release-slice` (C-10/P0.4)
and `host-slice` — and the map's own text admits the second on a stated ground:
`cache-slice.md:184` "already names as a boundary". So a slice may be added
without touching §7, provided an existing artifact names it as a boundary. The
bar is on inventing a *family*; adding a *slice* has precedent and a test.

## 3. The finding that decides it: the two gates disagree on what a slice is

`state-verdicts-discharge.md` is on disk, carries a cell→evidence map, and is
counted as a verification slice — by one gate:

| gate | rule | what it treats as a slice |
|---|---|---|
| `tools/deferral-gate.R` | D2, D4 | **every** `design/work/url-v3/verification/*.md` (`SLICE_GLOB_REL:46`, `list.files(..., "\\.md$"):137`) |
| `design/work/url-v3/tools/traceability-gate.R` | T5 | the fixed 10-name registry in the map's `## Verification slices` table |

Both gates pass today, and they contradict each other about the same file. D2
reports "every discharged deferral is claimed by a verification slice" — that
PASS depends on `state-verdicts-discharge.md` counting as a slice. T5 reports
"10 slices: 3 SHIPPED, 7 OWED, all agree with disk" — a census in which that
same file does not exist. The map's coverage census consequently reports the
verdict subject matter as having **no owning slice**, while the deferral gate
has already accepted a claim of coverage over it.

This is the real defect behind the issue, and it is not an ownership question
at all: **the traceability map is blind to discharge records.** Three exist
(`output-fsss-discharge.md`, `state-authority-discharge.md`,
`state-verdicts-discharge.md`); none appears anywhere in the map (zero hits for
`discharge`). The 62 `UNASSIGNED` claims therefore overstate the verification
gap, because the instrument that measures the gap cannot see one of the two
places evidence is recorded.

The overstatement is bounded and named: it is the 37, and only the 37.

## 4. The decision

**D-A — Split the disposition by shipped-ness, and answer only the shipped
half here.** The 62 are dispositioned as two sets, on the axis P0.5 already
uses. Nothing in this record assigns an unbuilt cell to a slice.

**D-B — The 37 shipped claims (VI s3–s6) go to `state-slice`.** The wider
reading in the issue is adopted, on the issue's own strongest argument and one
it did not have: these four sections elaborate the values and semantics of
`layer1_syntax_verdict`, `layer2_policy_verdict`, `layer3_annotation_state` and
`parse_status` — four fields `canonical-state-contract.md:137-140` already
owns, and which VI's own §Scope boundaries says it consumes "without renaming".
Splitting a field's *name* from its *value set* across two slices would put one
runtime property under two owners, which is the drift the single-writer rule
exists to prevent. `state-slice` therefore owns CS §Rows (44) **plus** VI s3–s6
(37) = 81 claims. It remains `OWED`; this fixes its scope, not its existence.

**D-C — The 25 unbuilt claims are deferral candidates, not slice candidates.**
They are `UNASSIGNED` because their surfaces do not exist, which under P0.5 is
a register question. The proposed carriers, one per surface, follow the
contract's own open cells rather than inventing destinations:

| claims | surface | proposed carrier |
|---|---|---|
| s1 (5), s7 (5), s8 (3) | the three-posture repair axis and everything provenance-bearing that depends on it | **VAL-O2** / RCON-05 — the public posture spelling |
| s2 (7) | the typed intervention ledger | **VAL-O1** / P2.6 (PROPOSED) for the categorization; the ledger surface itself is unbuilt and uncarried |
| s9 (5) | the resolver verdict surface | **VAL-O3** / RCON-03 |

Registering these as `VD-nnn` rows is deliberately **not** done by this record
(§6). The rows require a `family` value from the eight, and s1/s2/s7/s8 have no
natural family precisely because the input-repair surface is unbuilt — which is
the second question below, and the owner's to answer.

**D-D — No validation slice is created.** With the 37 owned by `state-slice`
and the 25 routed to the register, nothing is left for a ninth slice to own. A
slice created now would be an empty name certifying nothing — the exact finding
the map already reports against `vector-slice` (`## Open cells` 2) and the
oracle register reports against `browser-parity`. If the repair surface is
later built, D-C's carriers are where its slice question re-opens, with real
cells to own.

**D-E — Reconcile the two gates' definitions of "slice", in the direction of
the narrower one.** `deferral-gate.R`'s D2 currently accepts any
`verification/*.md` as a claimant, so a file named anything at all in that
directory satisfies a discharge. It should require the claimant to be either a
registered slice or a record that declares itself a discharge — and the map
should gain an explicit accounting of discharge records so its census stops
reporting evidence it cannot see as absent. **Both are executable changes and
neither is authored here**, because D2 is load-bearing for three currently
PASSING discharges and tightening it without the map change would turn a green
gate red for a reason unrelated to this issue. Carried as a follow-up (§6).

## 6. Non-scope

- **No contract cell is touched.** Every VI cell keeps its status and its
  `owner_decision_ref`. This record decides ownership of *evidence*, never
  semantics.
- **No map row is edited by this record.** `UNASSIGNED` is, in the map's own
  words, "a factual statement that no registered slice's family covers a
  section" and explicitly not a deferral. Flipping VI s3–s6 to `state-slice`
  while this record is PROPOSED would make the map assert an ownership the
  owner has not ratified — the same reasoning P2.6 §Consequences applies to its
  own cell, and P1.3's precedent. The map edit, the census regeneration, and
  the `state-slice` scope note ride the seal PR.
- **No `VD-nnn` row is registered.** Same reason, plus the open `family`
  question in D-C.
- **`deferral-gate.R` and `traceability-gate.R` are not modified** (D-E).
- **No implementation is scheduled.** Whether the repair posture, the
  intervention ledger, or the resolver verdict surface should be *built* is
  product authority (P2/RCON-05/RCON-03), untouched here.
- **G4's exit is not moved.** P0.5 governs that; this record only makes 62 of
  its claims legible.

## Open question this record does **not** answer

D-C routes 20 claims (s1, s2, s7, s8) to deferral rows that need a `family`
value from the fixed eight, and none fits an unbuilt *input-repair* surface —
`mutation` is the closest by the CM s6 precedent in §1(b), but CM s6 is
cleaning-side mutation and the input-repair pipeline is a different subject.
Forcing a family to make the row well-formed would be inventing the answer.
This is the residue of the original question, sharpened: **not** "which slice
owns validation", but "which of the eight families claims *input repair*, if
the surface is ever built". It belongs with whichever slice builds it.
