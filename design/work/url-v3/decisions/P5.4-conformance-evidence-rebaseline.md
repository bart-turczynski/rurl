# P5.4 — the conformance-evidence re-baseline

## Decision (PROPOSED)

> This record is the agent's **proposed** disposition, offered for the owner to
> ratify, reject, or adjust. Nothing here is binding until the owner merges it
> (P0.1 §4). It moves a **published number**, so it is deliberately a separate
> record rather than a note folded into P2.5.

## 1. The defect this closes

Every conformance and benchmark harness in the repository scored `clean_url`.
Per `output-contracts.md` (P2.2 §1c/§5.1), `clean_url` is output surface (c):
"a policy-driven SEO/canonicalization product; **not** a serializer, identity,
redirect target, or conformance oracle." P5.3 CLAIM-1 does not admit it as a
claim substrate.

It was not a careless choice. Surface (b) did not exist, so there was nothing
admissible to score against; the harnesses predate it. P2.5 shipped the
serializer, and this record re-baselines the evidence onto it.

**The corpus SHAPE was distorted too, not just the comparison.** Before this
pass the 353-row external corpus carried **zero** expected values with a
fragment or credentials, and the 75-row conformance fixture contained **zero**
`@` characters. That is a consequence, not an oversight: those are exactly the
rows a `clean_url` comparison could never have passed, because surface (c) drops
both by contract. Any such row would have failed the moment it was added, so the
corpus grew into the shape its harness could score.

## 2. Two axes, two headlines — and 158/99 is not replaced

The single most important clarification in this record. There are **two**
conformance questions, they have different authorities and different substrates,
and the published 158/99 answers only one of them.

| | axis 1 | axis 2 |
|---|---|---|
| question | does rurl emit the standard's **serialization**? | does rurl **accept/reject** what the grammar does? |
| authority | `whatwg-wpt` (WPT, Ada) | `rfc3986-grammar` (the ABNF referee) |
| substrate | `serialize_url()` output string | acceptance only |
| the old figure | *did not exist* | **158/99**, later 164/93 |

**158/99 is an axis-2 number.** P5.3 §2.2 says so explicitly and adds that it is
"**not** the FSSS full-string conformance headline (which is per §1 asserted on
`serialize_url()` and whose suites are still to be built)". This record builds
those suites. Axis 1 is therefore **new evidence**, not a restatement — and any
report presenting it as "158/99, corrected" would be wrong.

It was also already stale before this pass: `analysis/parity/README.md` records
**164/93** after `RURL-qrfrvmkg` closed the `RURL-pfewxbhb` coverage gap.

### 2.1 Axis 1 — the FSSS full-string headline (NEW)

Over the 396-row external fixture (325 runnable), reported **by substrate**,
because most rows carry only a verdict and an aggregate over them would measure
rejection, not serialization (P5.3 §2.2):

| substrate | oracle kinds | rows | conforming | documented deviations |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **string-valued** | `exact`, `host` | 92 | **91** | 1 |
| verdict-only | `failure`, `accept` | 233 | 232 | 1 |

Stated in the P5.3 §2.2 form:

> **91 conforming / 1 documented deviation against `whatwg-wpt`, WHATWG URL
> Standard full serialization, over the 92 string-valued rows of
> `external-url-vectors.csv`.**

The deviations are `eq-U8` (UTS-46 folds U+0130 into the host before the
authority split; rurl splits at `@` first) and, on the verdict substrate,
`yal-009` (WHATWG reads a dotted `www.php.net` as a **scheme** with an opaque
path; rurl rejects).

### 2.2 Axis 2 — RFC 3986 grammar acceptance (MOVED)

Like-for-like on the same 257-row oracle-carrying scope the published figure
uses, changing only the surface:

| surface | conforming | departures | rejects-what-RFC-admits | accepts-what-RFC-forbids |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `clean_url` (c) — published | 164 | 93 | 81 | 12 |
| `serialize_url()` (b) | **179** | **78** | 66 | 12 |

Fifteen rows move. Every one is a case where surface (c) declined **by policy**
— the ADR 0004 closed scheme set, the ADR 0002 reversible Unicode host — and
surface (b) matches the grammar. rurl's parser never disagreed with the RFC on
any of them.

Over the **new** 325-runnable scope (after the credential/fragment slice of §3)
the figure is **235 conforming / 90 departures** (70 rejects-what-RFC-admits, 20
accepts-what-RFC-forbids). The two scopes are reported separately and never
mixed: the 15-row improvement is the like-for-like claim, and 235/90 is the
current whole-corpus state.

## 3. The shape gap is closed with upstream oracles, not hand-authored ones

43 rows added from the committed WPT import (`inst/bench/wpt-url-cases.json`,
BSD-3-Clause), whose recorded components are assembled into an expected `href`
by a transcription of the WHATWG URL serializer (URL Standard §4.5) sharing no
code with rurl: 21 credentials, 6 empty-credentials, 14 fragment, 2
fragment+empty-query. rurl matches all 43 exactly. The string-valued substrate
nearly doubles, 49 → 92.

**This settles P2.5 open question 1 by evidence.** The contested rows
(`http://@www.example.com`, `http://:@www.example.com`, `https://:@test`) are in
WPT with the credentials dropped, delimiter and all — WHATWG's "includes
credentials" is false when both halves are empty. That is the standard's own
answer, not an argument from first principles, and it is what rurl does. The
apparent tension with the contract's "credential-complete" language resolves the
way the standing spec-exactness mandate requires: completeness lives in the
**record**, and the undivided spelling is rendered verbatim by the RFC `source`
posture.

## 4. What else changed, and why it is not merely a column swap

1. **Scoring happens once.** Six per-source builders each answered "does rurl
   meet this row's oracle?" against the wrong surface in a slightly *different*
   way — exact match, substring containment, NA-check. That is how a mismatch
   could hide in one harness and not another. `diverges` and `fsss_conforms` are
   now derived from one definition in `build-oracle-columns.R`.

2. **Host oracles compare an extracted host, not a substring.** The old harness
   asked `grepl(oracle, clean_url, fixed = TRUE)`. On a hostname-confusion
   corpus that is the wrong question: both hostnames appear in
   `http://letsencrypt.org%2F@malware.testing.google.test/`, so containment
   passes whether the parse is correct or **inverted** — it could not detect the
   confusion the corpus exists to detect.

3. **The conformance fixture's `divergence_class` is derived from the
   NORMALIZED form** (P2.5 OUT-O3), not the default `source`. On `source` the
   class is wrong in both directions, and 7 of 75 rows were misclassified.

4. **The C-04 conflation is fixed at its stated site.**
   `test-url-standard-conformance.R` claimed the WHATWG profile matches "the URL
   Standard serializer" while every assertion compared `expected_clean_url`. The
   claim now has an assertion: `expected_serialized`.

## 5. Guardrails kept, and one that caught this pass

The RURL-nknytzxz two-axis split is preserved exactly: `divergence_class` stays
standard-vs-standard, `rurl_deviation` stays rurl-vs-standard, and the RFC
oracle is derived from the ABNF referee — **never** from rurl.

That guard earned its keep here. An earlier draft of `build-oracle-columns.R`
derived `rfc3986_expected` from `serialize_url(standard = "rfc3986")`, recording
rurl's **policy** rejections as claims about what the standard requires. The
ABNF referee in `test-external-url-vectors.R` caught it on 66 rows. That is
precisely the co-confirmation trap the audit exists to prevent, and it fired.

Per-row RFC ownership from `tools/oracle-audit-rfc3986.R` is preserved, not
re-authored. This pass may only **retire** a deviation the FSSS no longer has.
Rows added after that audit are attributed **by family**, from the audit's own
two buckets, and a departure fitting neither stops the build rather than being
absorbed into an existing owner.

## 6. Three serializer defects found and fixed by this pass

Two were introduced by the FSSS itself (`df00da8`/`1b531d2`) and were invisible
to every existing test, because `clean_url` drops userinfo:

* `.fsss_source_lex()` read the **raw** source where the parser reads the
  backslash-rewritten one, so path text before the last `@` became a phantom
  userinfo and the host was duplicated into the credentials:
  `http://google.com:80\@yahoo.com` → `http://google.com:80%5C@google.com/@yahoo.com`.
* `.has_explicit_authority()` greps a literal `://`, which an input using
  WHATWG's special-authority-ignore-slashes state need not contain, so the FSSS
  **dropped the host**: `https:/\/\/\github.com/foo/bar` → `https:/foo/bar`.

Both are fixed (`78c7667`, `592d49b`) with regression tests. Every affected row
— the CVE-2020-26291 hostname-confusion family — now agrees with its oracle.

The third is **pre-existing and filed, not fixed here**: under `rfc3986` the
host is percent-decoded past the unreserved set, so the serializer's own output
no longer re-parses, and `http://ho%7Fst/` emits a raw DEL byte that renders as
a clean hostname. It is a Stage-A/libcurl behavior (`get_host()` has always
returned `ho|st`), so per the standing "file against the parser, do not
compensate in the serializer" precedent it does not belong in this pass.

## 7. Consequences

* `NEWS.md`, `P5.3 §2.2`, `registers/oracle-register.md` (OR-002) and
  `analysis/parity/README.md` are updated to state both axes with their
  authority, substrate and fixture scope.
* OR-002's scope line must stop implying its count is *the* conformance
  headline; it is the axis-2 acceptance count, and axis 1 now exists.
* The G4 finding "every conformance claim rides `clean_url`" is discharged.
* `yal-009` and the `urn:ietf:rfc:2648` case (P2.5 open question 3) are the same
  family — the RFC `path-rootless`/no-authority production rurl's authority
  model does not implement. They should be filed together against the parser.

## Open questions

1. **Does the owner accept 91/1 as a publishable headline?** It is a small
   substrate (92 rows). It is honest and upstream-derived, but it is not a broad
   conformance claim and should not be quoted without its scope.
2. **Should axis 2 be re-audited at the new scope?**
   `tools/oracle-audit-rfc3986.R` last ran over 282 rows; the corpus is now 325
   runnable. The family attribution added here is a stopgap that refuses
   unattributable rows, not a re-audit.
