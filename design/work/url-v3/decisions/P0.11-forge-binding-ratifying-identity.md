# P0.11 — ratification reconstituted on GitLab

## Decision (PROPOSED)

> This record is the agent's **proposed** disposition, offered for the owner to
> ratify, reject, or adjust. Nothing here is binding until the owner merges it
> (P0.1 §4). §3 states plainly that merging it is an extraordinary act rather
> than an ordinary application of P0.1, and why that is the honest description.

### 1. The defect

P0.1 §Decision(2) reads:

> This identity is durably bound to the forge account **`github:bart-turczynski`**,
> whose merge (or a merge it authorizes) supplies the approval evidence in
> §Ratification; the record and the account that ratifies it are therefore the
> same authority.

and P0.1 §Ratification step 2 reads:

> **Ratify.** The owner (`github:bart-turczynski`) merges the PR. That merge is
> the acceptance.

That account has been suspended by the platform. Push, fetch, API and merge all
return 403, restoration is uncertain and not time-bounded, and `origin` is now
`git@gitlab.com:bart-turczynski/rurl.git`. The owner is unchanged and in full
control of the repository; the named evidence channel is simply gone.

The consequence under the accepted mechanism is total: **no record can be
ratified at all.** Ten records sit `PROPOSED` (P0.6, P0.7, P0.8, P0.9, P0.10,
P1.3, P2.5, P2.6, P3.3, P5.4); G3 cannot be re-accepted; G4 exit and G5 final
acceptance cannot close.

P0.1 §Consequences anticipates growth — "a future multi-approver need is a new
P0-tier record, not an edit" — but it anticipates *growth*, not the failure of
the mechanism's own precondition. The unavailability of the sole ratifying
identity is a case P0.1 does not cover, and §3 is honest about what follows.

### 2. The decision

**2.1 Authority and authentication are separated.** The sole owner and approver
remains **Bart Turczynski `<bartek@turczynski.pl>`**, acting as repository
owner. A forge account is a means of *authenticating* that the owner acted; it
is not the owner. P0.1 wrote the two as one thing because there was one forge
and no reason to distinguish them. From this record forward they are distinct
fields with distinct failure modes: the owner is durable, an account is not.

**2.2 Ratifying identities are an ordered set, bound by immutable identifiers.**

| Order | Forge | Immutable id | Label (mutable, informational) | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | GitLab | `27694267` | `bart-turczynski` | **primary** — `origin`; where merges happen today |
| 2 | GitHub | `142225707` | `bart-turczynski` | retained and valid; currently suspended |

The binding is to the **numeric account id**, not the username. A username is a
display name the holder can change and a later party can claim; the numeric id
cannot be reassigned. P0.1's binding was to a username, which is why this table
is the durable form of the same intent. The GitHub id is evidenced inside this
repository by the commit trailer
`142225707+bart-turczynski@users.noreply.github.com`.

Rules:

1. A merge to `main` performed by **any identity in this set** is a ratifying act
   and moves the merged record from `PROPOSED` to `ACCEPTED`.
2. `accepted_evidence` must name **the merge commit SHA, the forge, and the
   numeric identity** that performed it, e.g.
   `<sha> (gitlab:27694267, MR !N, squash-merge into main)`. P0.1's format
   recorded only the SHA because the forge was implicit; it no longer is, and
   evidence that does not say where and by whom is not reconstructible from the
   repository alone.
3. Adding or removing an identity requires a superseding P0-tier record. The set
   is not a configuration value an agent may edit.
4. An identity being *unavailable* neither removes it from the set nor
   invalidates anything it previously ratified. Entry 2 stays: every record
   ACCEPTED before this one was ratified by it, and all of them remain valid.

**2.3 A seal must record the verification it actually stands on.** On GitHub the
ratifying merge carried the gate implicitly: the twelve workflows in
`.github/workflows/` ran on the PR, so "the owner merged it" entailed "it was
verified." On GitLab that entailment is **false** — none of those workflows run
there and there is no `.gitlab-ci.yml`. A merge on `origin` today proves only
that the owner clicked merge.

While the ratifying forge runs no server-side gate, a seal commit must record the
verification actually performed — the command and its result, e.g.
`Rscript tools/verify.R` with pass/fail counts — alongside the merge evidence.

**This is a disclosure duty, and disclosure is second-best.** The better fix is
to restore a server-side gate on the ratifying forge, at which point a merge
again entails the verification and this clause becomes redundant rather than
wrong; it then expires on its own terms. Ordering this clause *after* a working
GitLab gate rather than before it would remove the exposed interval instead of
merely documenting it, at the cost of leaving the ten records blocked for
longer. The owner may prefer that sequencing; if so, strike §2.3, land the CI
record first, and this record follows it.

**2.4 The ten pending records are ratified individually, and only prospectively.**

1. No bulk state change. Each of the ten is merged on its own, retaining its own
   proposal commit and its own merge evidence per §2.2(2).
2. The applicable checks are re-run at the time of each merge. A check that
   passed months ago on a different forge is not evidence for a merge today.
3. Acceptance dates from the merge that confers it. Nothing is backdated, and no
   record is treated as having been ACCEPTED during the period when no
   ratification was possible. That period is a gap in the acceptance history and
   is recorded as one.

### 3. This is a reconstitution, not a continuation — and the record says so

The tempting framing is that P0.1 already located authority in the owner, so
ratifying on another forge merely uses an evidence channel P0.1 did not name.
**That framing is not adopted here, because it does not survive P0.1's own
words.** §Decision(2) says the identity is "durably bound" to the named account
and that "the record and the account that ratifies it are therefore the same
authority", and §Ratification step 2 names that account as the actor. Read
together, the account is constitutive of the procedure, not merely descriptive
of it. On that reading a GitLab merge does not satisfy P0.1, and a record
claiming otherwise would be asserting a continuity that does not exist.

There is also a structural reason not to adopt it: P0.1's text is genuinely
ambiguous, and the reading that unblocks the work is the one the party proposing
the record benefits from. An ambiguous authority mechanism should not be
resolved in the proposer's favour by the proposer.

So this record makes the weaker and truer claim:

- **The owner's authority over this repository is not derived from P0.1.** P0.1
  is an instrument the owner adopted to record decisions; the owner's capacity
  to govern the repository precedes it and survives it.
- **P0.1's *procedure* cannot produce this record's acceptance.** Its
  precondition — an available bound identity — has failed, and it provides no
  recovery path.
- **Therefore merging this record is an extraordinary owner act that
  reconstitutes the ratification mechanism**, not an ordinary amendment passed
  under the old rule. The discontinuity is real, is dated, and is recorded here
  rather than papered over.

What this costs, stated plainly: the acceptance chain has a documented break at
this record. A later reviewer will see that P0.11 was not accepted by P0.1's
procedure. That is the honest state of affairs and is strictly better than a
chain that *looks* unbroken because the break was described away.

**If GitHub is restored**, the owner may — but is not required to — ratify a
bridge record from `github:142225707` affirming this reconstitution and the
records accepted under it. That would close the break prospectively. It must not
backdate anything.

**The alternative the owner may prefer: reject this record.** If the owner reads
P0.1 as admitting no reconstitution short of restoring the original account, the
correct response is an appeal to GitHub and continued deadlock until it
succeeds — option 1 in RURL-wsphrtjc. That is a coherent position with a real
cost (indefinite deadlock, and an unresolved single point of failure if the
appeal succeeds), and it is the owner's to take.

### 4. What this record does not do

- **It does not claim continuity with P0.1's procedure.** See §3.
- **It does not retroactively validate anything.** No act performed while
  ratification was impossible becomes valid by this record. Acceptance dates
  from the conferring merge (§2.4.3).
- **It does not re-stamp existing records.** Every currently ACCEPTED record was
  ratified by `github:142225707` at a time when that identity was available and
  bound. Those acceptances stand; no `accepted_evidence` changes, no record is
  INVALIDATED, and no gate reopens on account of this record.
- **It does not add an approver.** The set in §2.2 is two accounts held by one
  person. Multi-approver authority remains what P0.1 §Consequences says it is: a
  future P0-tier record that does not exist.
- **It does not delegate.** P0.1 §Decision(4) — agent proposes, owner accepts, no
  agent action confers authority — stands untouched. An agent may open the merge
  request; only the owner may merge it.
- **It does not change what a seal is.** P0.1 §Ratification's two-phase procedure
  (propose → ratify → seal on its own branch, never a direct push to `main`) is
  unchanged; §2.2(2) and §2.3 extend only what the seal *records*.
- **It does not restore a server-side gate**, and must not be read as accepting
  that one is unnecessary. See §2.3.
- **It touches no contract, no product semantics and no public surface.**

### 5. Recovery, so this cannot recur

The situation this record addresses was reachable because the mechanism had
exactly one identity channel and no stated behaviour for its loss. §2.2 makes the
set plural, which mitigates but does not close that: both entries are accounts on
third-party platforms, either of which may be suspended.

A durable recovery mechanism — commit/tag signing with a key the owner holds
independently of any forge, sufficient on its own to ratify — is **not decided
here**. It is a real design question with its own trade-offs (key custody,
verification tooling, what happens when the key is lost) and folding it into an
unblocking record would be the same scope creep §2.3 flags. It is recorded as
owed work under RURL-wsphrtjc.

### 6. Numbering

This record takes **P0.11** rather than the next free number visible from `main`.
P0.7, P0.8, P0.9 and P0.10 exist on `feat/v3-protocol-hardening` and are not
reachable from `main`; naming this record P0.7 would pass `validate-records.R` on
this branch and produce a `[duplicate_ids]` failure the moment the integration
line rebases onto the merged result. The gap between P0.6 and P0.11 on `main` is
deliberate and is not a missing record.

## Ratification and lifecycle stamping

Per P0.1 §Ratification's form, with the actor as reconstituted by §2.2 and §3:

1. **Propose.** This record lands on `docs/p0.11-forge-binding` at
   `state: PROPOSED`, `accepted_at` / `accepted_evidence` `pending`. The merge
   request is the proposal.
2. **Ratify.** The owner merges it from `gitlab:27694267`. Per §3 this is an
   extraordinary reconstituting act, not an ordinary application of P0.1.
3. **Seal.** A subsequent bookkeeping commit, on its own branch via its own merge
   request, sets `state: ACCEPTED`, stamps `accepted_at` and `accepted_evidence`
   (SHA + forge + numeric identity), registers the record in `manifest.yaml`
   `decisions[]` with its post-stamp `sha256`, and records the verification
   evidence §2.3 requires.

**Known validator state while PROPOSED.** `validate-manifest.R` rule 7 requires
every `decisions/*.md` on disk to be referenced in `manifest.yaml decisions[]`
with `state: ACCEPTED`. This record is deliberately **not** pre-registered — a
PROPOSED record listed as ACCEPTED would be a false claim, and registration is
the seal's job. So `validate-manifest` reports
`decision record on disk not referenced in manifest: P0.11-forge-binding-ratifying-identity.md`
until the seal lands. That is the same expected state P0.6 currently carries on
the integration branch; it is not a defect and must not be "fixed" by
pre-stamping.
